# Useful Definitions


It's useful to define 'definitions' optimally for intents like 'identifying examples and matching/complying/implementing structures' and 'identifying differing structures from the defined concept'.


## Examples

The following are examples of 'useful definitions':
- it's useful to define 'evil' with generalizations/specifications like 'abusing power' or 'easy cruelty' or 'opposing good'
- it's useful to define 'find' as a set of specific functions like 'filter/identify/define/specify/match'
- it's useful to define 'relevance' as a 'set of optimized interactions between a core set of relevance components and general problem types'

Graph definitions of 'concepts' are similarly useful:
- the graph of the definition of 'evil' is useful to identify, to implement/describe a connection like 'pure evil is simpler than all other vectors, and all other vectors are superior to pure evil, so pure evil lacks complexity/variation'
- the graph of the 'find' function like a graph of similar functions is useful to identify 'function clusters/types'
- the graph of the 'relevance' definition is useful to identify 'causes/optimizations/implementations/specifications/interactions of structures/combinations of relevance components'

Definitions standardized to 'optimality/relevance' are similarly useful:
- defining evil in terms of 'optimality' results in a definition like 'a suboptimal outdated optimization as in "minimizing work" that is rarely optimal and only for suboptimal intents'
- defining 'find' in terms of 'optimality' results in a definition related to interactions like 'optimality filters' and otherwise 'defining/filtering/identifying optimality'
- defining 'relevance' in terms of 'optimality' is trivial bc theyre already connected by 'conceptual/general similarity' and 'optimality can be formatted as a component of relevance' (relevance/optimality/usefulness/meaning are similarly connected conceptually), though 'relevance' also means 'importance/uniqueness/other components' and 'optimality' means 'the upper limit of possible ideal structures' so they trivially differ

Otherwise standardizing concepts to other interfaces is similarly useful, to 'completely define the concept' by 'connecting it to every independent variable (every interface)'.


## Relevance Interactions

These are 'useful definitions' bc they 'interact with relevance (like by implementing relevance)'.

The 'useful/relevant/optimal/meaningful definition' is the 'interactive definition' which is 'usefully generalized/specified/otherwise varied' to identify its 'important/optimal interactions' with relevance structures like 'general problem types'.

With a 'useful definition of relevance', problem-solving can be automated (like with the 'definition that usefully implements/optimizes interactions of every relevant graph, and the optimized interactions of relevant graphs with general problem types').

'Definitions' are a structure of 'certainty/structure', which makes them useful for 'automation', and applying them with 'opposing' structures like 'variation' is similarly useful for intents like 'generate relevant variation' which are inputs to optimization intents like 'increase relevance'.
