# Generating Biological Intelligence

What would it take to 'change a suboptimal thinker into an independent super genius'?


## Difference/Problem Definition

The 'differences between these mind types' is useful to identify:
- the difference between 'one simple graph' and 'all of the thousands of relevant graphs/algorithms/connections/definitions/variables/other structures Ive identified'
- a thousand days of 'thinking about those relevant structures', like 'thinking to grow/apply new/inactive neurons to comprehend a specific relevant structure'
- a thousand applications of a 'thousand days of thinking', like thinking to 'identify new relevant structures' or 'optimize/integrate/organize relevant structures'

Specifically, relevant graphs/structures include:
- the useful specification of the graph of the definition of optimality/relevance
- a graph of perspectives indicating error perspectives as 'clearly/simply different' from solution perspectives
- the intersecting spectrum concept combination/structure graph

This means a 'billion units of mental work' is required to change a suboptimal thinker into a super genius (without additional resources like 'injections of new neurons' or 'access to the internet/LLMs'). By comparison, 'changing a very smart person into a super genius' likely requires 'only a thousand, or a hundred thousand, units of mental work' (they can likely just 'read my blog, and spend a thousand days thinking about thinking/optimization/interface structures (with intent to implement interface/optimized thinking)' and become a 'similarly independent thinker' as a super genius).


## Connection/solution examples

Examples of 'connections between a suboptimal/super genius brain' include:
- the sequence/network of my inventions (or 'optimized variants of this sequence/network')
- relevant interface structures like 'graphs' of thinking structures like 'minds'
- a sequence/network of increasingly relevant/complex problems to build an independent brain by 'generating problem sequences with a decreasing amount of info available to solve each subsequent problem' (thereby increasing the requirement for independence)
