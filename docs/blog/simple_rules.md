# Simple rules 

It's useful to identify 'simple rules to follow' or 'simple sanity checks' while 'waiting on interface analysis to be completed/applied correctly'.


## Examples

### A simple rule applying the concept of 'ultimate understanding/relevance'

Generating/increasing 'understanding' of a tech like 'quantum entanglement' and 'quantum brain functions' can cause 'chaos cascades' (a 'possible meaning' of that function is the 'end of time/reality'). 

So it's useful to always/regularly/iteratively apply interface analysis, to 'calculate this relevance or these meanings' of what intents/functions you're implementing/applying, to offset our default 'lack of understanding' of the 'meaning of our actions' as much as possible. It is a real possibility that we will never understand the ultimate meaning of our actions (like 'it is not know what impact/meaning a chaos cascade in this universe would have on other universes'), but by 'applying interface analysis to the set of relevant graphs' and applying interface analysis to other graphs like 'graphs of current actions/understanding', we can try to approximate or perceive a unit of the ultimate meaning.

A simple rule to apply 'ultimate understanding' is to:
- 'dont do anything you dont understand, to some useful ratio of completeness (if you understand a system to the point where you can easily predict its 'interactions with interface variables or general problem types', then you understand it to a useful ratio of completeness, but you still don't have 'ultimate understanding of the meaning of that system', which requires a 'high ratio of computation to identify')

An example of applying this rule is to:
- avoid reducing the ratio of smart people (to maximize the people who understand the most)
- learn/apply interface analysis (to maximize understanding)
- maximize independent thinking (to maximize 'valuable/legitimate understanding/criticism, like by identifying optimal alternatives' caused by 'independence')

Another related simple rule:
- if you cant 'prove that something wont end reality, harm other universes, cause chaos cascades, or trivialize/cause other negative intents', don't do it

Our prediction/proof/analysis tools are quite good now, and the 'set of relevant graphs' will improve them, so we should be able to prove the 'ultimate meaning' of an intent/function sometime soon, possibly within a few years ('proving it' by 'identifying its structure on relevant graphs', like 'proving that in every independent case, it will not cause negative intents to be trivialized/applied/implemented'). At that point, there will be drastically reduced risk in applying intents like 'implement a technology' and 'distribute the technology'.


### A simple set of sanity checks

It's useful to regularly apply simple rules, like the following 'interactions with interface variables':
- interaction with 'groups': who are we helping?
- interaction with 'truth': have we applied a high ratio of facts recently?
- interaction with 'meaning': has this increased our understanding (like 'optimized a relevant graph')?
- interaction with 'relevant variation': has this increased time/relevant variation, has it solved more problems than it causes, did this increase the ratio of solution variation to problem variation so we are staying ahead of problems?
